Why frontier AI models get exponentially more expensive

2.4× / yearTraining compute spendBigger clusters running for longer
5× / yearTraining compute (FLOP)Larger models + more training data
~3× / yearEnergy usageGigawatt-scale data centers
Each new frontier model needs to be significantly more capable than the last, requiring dramatically more resources. On current trends, the largest training runs pass $1 billion around 2026–27 — and the race has widened beyond single runs to infrastructure: OpenAI alone plans roughly $50B of compute spending in 2026.

Why AI gets ~4× cheaper each year

1.4× / yearBetter hardwareComputing power per dollar keeps improving — Moore's Law plus chip efficiency gains
3× / yearSmarter algorithmsThe same capability needs less computation — better training methods, better data, more "thinking" at inference time
1.4×  ×  3×  ≈  4× more affordable every year. This is why capabilities don't stay exclusive: a run that only a tech giant could fund becomes a startup project within a few years — and why Epoch finds frontier-level performance typically reaches consumer hardware within about a year.

This page is an updated rebuild of Dewi Erwan's original "Trends in AI: Training Costs and Diffusion Speed," refreshed with the 2025–26 model generation. Cost figures use compute cost per training run (hardware-time or cloud-rental basis, which differ ~2×); they exclude staff and experiments. Points for models released after mid-2025 are order-of-magnitude estimates — no developer has disclosed figures. The 4×/year diffusion rate is a long-run average; the true rate varies by capability and has plausibly been faster in the reasoning-model era.
